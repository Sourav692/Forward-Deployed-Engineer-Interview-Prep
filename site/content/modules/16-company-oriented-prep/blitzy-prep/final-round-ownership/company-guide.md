# Ownership Story Workbook: Final Round

Ownership is not saying "I took charge." It is showing that you made ambiguity visible, took the next responsible action, brought the right people into the decision, and stayed accountable for the result.

| Result, evidence, and measurement limits |  |  |  |

A story is ready when you can answer "what did you personally do?", "who disagreed?", and "how do you know it worked?" without adding invented detail. If a result was qualitative, say how it was observed or confirmed.

## 1. Pick three stories with different proof

| Story type | What it proves | Useful questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambiguous problem | You can create clarity without waiting for perfect instructions | "Tell me about a time the goal was unclear." |
| Pushback | You can disagree without becoming defensive | "Tell me about a time you challenged a senior stakeholder." |
| Delivery issue or mistake | You own outcomes after the plan breaks | "Tell me about a failure or difficult situation." |

Use a fourth story only as backup. A final-round answer improves through rehearsal, not by carrying ten half-prepared examples.

## 2. The ownership story structure

| Part | Include | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | The customer, outcome, scale, and why it mattered | A long company history |
| Ambiguity | What was unknown, contested, or blocked | Pretending the answer was obvious |
| Your responsibility | The decision you owned | "We did" without saying what you did |
| Action | How you investigated, aligned people, chose a path, and communicated | A list of meetings without decisions |
| Result | Outcome, measure, and residual risk | Inflated impact or a number you cannot explain |
| Learning | A specific operating change you made afterwards | "I learned communication is important" |

## 3. Write a 120-second version

For each story, complete these prompts in plain language.

1. **Headline:** "I owned a situation where ..."
2. **Stakes:** "It mattered because ..."
3. **The hard part:** "The ambiguity or disagreement was ..."
4. **My move:** "I first ..., then ..., and I made the decision by ..."
5. **How I handled people:** "I involved ... because ..., and I told ... early because ..."
6. **Result:** "The result was ..."
7. **What changed afterwards:** "Afterwards, I changed ... so that ..."

Now create a 45-second version. Keep the headline, judgement, action, and result. Drop supporting detail first.

## 4. Pushback without becoming adversarial

Use this pattern when an executive or customer wants a riskier, broader, or faster path.

> "I understand the outcome you want is <outcome>. I see two credible ways to get there. Option A gets us there faster, but creates <specific risk>. Option B gives us evidence sooner and limits that risk, but costs <specific trade-off>. My recommendation is B because <reason>. If you choose A, I will make the controls and contingency explicit rather than pretending the risk disappeared."

This shows three important behaviours: you respect the business goal, you put a real choice on the table, and you stay accountable whichever path is chosen.

## 5. Difficult follow-ups to practise

| Follow-up | A good answer does |
| --- | --- |
| "What would you have done if they still disagreed?" | States your recommendation, records the risk, then commits to making the agreed path successful |
| "How did you know you were right?" | Names the evidence, customer signal, experiment, or metric, rather than intuition alone |
| "What did you personally do?" | Separates your decision and contribution from the team outcome |
| "What was the cost of your choice?" | Names a trade-off honestly |
| "What did you do when the plan failed?" | Shows containment, communication, learning, and a changed system or process |

## 6. Final-round ownership scorecard

After each rehearsal, score the answer from 0 to 2.

| Check | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specificity | Vague | Some context | Clear stakes and your role |
| Judgement | No decision | Decision stated | Trade-off and evidence stated |
| Ownership | Team-only language | Your role implied | Your action and accountability are explicit |
| Stakeholder handling | No people context | Mentions people | Explains alignment, disagreement, or communication |
| Outcome | No result | Result claimed | Defensible result and learning |

A useful target is 8 or more out of 10. Do not optimise for a perfect script. Optimise for a clear, true answer.

## 7. Blitzy connection

Where natural, relate your operating style to the FDE role:

- You would not promise an autonomous outcome before the customer has a safe validation path.
- You would name review capacity, environment health, and test reliability as delivery constraints early.
- You would treat product gaps as information to bring back to the product team, not a problem to hide from the customer.
- You would measure customer value and adoption, not just generated code or activity.
