# Blitzy Final Round: Runbook

This round tests three things at once: engineering judgement, ownership in ambiguity, and executive communication. The goal is not to give three unrelated answers. Show the same operating style throughout: understand the real problem, make the trade-off explicit, take responsibility for the outcome, and keep the people affected informed.

## The message to leave behind

> "I can get into a messy customer environment, make the technical work safe and concrete, give senior people an honest decision, and stay with the outcome after the first deploy."

Do not memorise that sentence. Use it to check whether your examples and answers tell the same story.

## Reuse the Repository Before Rehearsing

The final round builds on the earlier rounds. Use the existing guides to refresh the reasoning, then use this folder to practise answers grounded in your experience. The reading times below are suggested skim budgets, not full-document reading estimates.

| Interview section | Start with existing material | Produce before the mock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering and technical aptitude | Blitzy brownfield integration and testing, incident triage, and the end-to-end FDE execution review; see the engineering guide's reading path | One real project walkthrough, one changed technical decision, and a production-readiness explanation |
| Ownership | Hiring-manager worksheets for ambiguity, pushback, and delivery pressure; see the ownership guide's worksheet map | Three real stories with your actions, evidence, outcome, and learning |
| Executive communication | Blitzy CTO trust and roadmap, stakeholder influence, and the delivery framework; see the executive guide's reading path | One scope negotiation, one bad-news update, and one decision request |

Start with your weakest section. Read only the listed sections, close the source, and explain the decision aloud. Return to a source when a follow-up exposes a gap.

## A Focused Three-Hour Prep Plan

| Block | Time | Material | Done when you can |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical refresh | 35 min | Engineering guide: essential reading path, then one real project walkthrough | Give a 90-second approach answer and defend one technical trade-off |
| Choose stories | 45 min | Ownership guide: worksheet map and three-story capture sheet | Tell three real stories with defensible outcomes and one lesson each |
| Practise scope conversations | 35 min | Executive guide: essential reading path and stakeholder evidence sheet | Explain a scope trade-off in 60 seconds and name the decision owner |
| Rehearse under pressure | 50 min | Mock interview, including its reflection segment | Complete the mock aloud and score each answer |
| Tighten | 15 min | Mock remediation map and your notes | Repair the weakest answer and repeat it in 45 seconds |

Keep one page of personal notes: three story headlines, your decisions, measured results, the trade-offs, and two questions for the panel. If you have not measured a result, describe the evidence you do have; do not manufacture a number.

## Your three-story portfolio

Bring three different stories. They should be real, specific, and safe to share without client-confidential information.

| Story | Best use | The proof you need ready |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambiguous customer problem | Ownership and technical judgement | What was unclear, how you made it measurable, and the result |
| Pushback or a difficult trade-off | Executive communication and judgement | The options, the risk you named, who decided, and what happened |
| Delivery problem or mistake | Ownership and credibility | How you stabilised the situation, communicated, and changed the system afterwards |

One story can answer several questions, but do not force every answer back to one engagement. The panel will notice.

## Final-round answer shape

Use this sequence for almost every answer:

1. **Headline:** the conclusion or action you took.
2. **Context:** enough scope to understand the stakes.
3. **Judgement:** the constraint or trade-off that mattered.
4. **Action:** what you personally did, in order.
5. **Result:** the outcome, with a defensible measure where possible.
6. **Learning:** what you would repeat or change.

For a technical question, add the evidence you would use to decide. For an executive question, add the decision you needed from them.

## What to avoid

- A long architecture tour before stating the customer outcome.
- Claiming you "led" work without naming your decision or action.
- Invented numbers or outcomes. Say what you measured and what you did not know.
- Making a stakeholder sound unreasonable. Describe the pressure they were under.
- Treating a scope reduction as failure. A narrower scope can be the responsible way to deliver value.
- Repeating product claims you cannot explain. It is stronger to say, "My understanding is X. I would validate that with the team."

## Night-before checklist

- Can you explain why Blitzy's output still needs customer review and validation?
- Can you tell each of your three stories in two minutes and in 45 seconds?
- Can you describe a time you gave bad news early?
- Can you say what you would do in your first 30 days with a new customer?
- Can you ask two thoughtful questions that build on the interviewer's answers?
