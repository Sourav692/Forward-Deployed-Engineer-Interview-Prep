# Roll Out, Operate, and Prove the Result

## Module 5: Roll Out, Operate, and Prove the Result

Shipping is not the finish line. Delivery is the result the system produces in real use.

&#91;embedded content: Module 5 infographic\]

### Topic 5.1: Choose first users and a way back

Before launch, answer these:

- Who gets the feature first?
- Can it be switched on and off with a feature flag?
- Which numbers will we watch?
- What conditions trigger a rollback?
- Who is on hand if something fails?

### Topic 5.2: Watch the workflow, not just the servers

Healthy servers do not guarantee a healthy product. Track three kinds of signals:

| Kind | What to watch |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | Error rate, speed, queue length, AI timeouts, payment notification failures |
| Product | Completion rate, drop-off, successful bookings, finished interview sessions |
| Business | Time to resolve, support cost, conversions, retention, customer satisfaction |

### Topic 5.3: Turn production failures into planned work

- The loop is: Ship, Observe, Learn, Prioritise, Improve.
- Real use will prove parts of your original plan wrong. That is normal.
- Before fixing a failure, sort it: was it product design, code, data, operations, search/retrieval, AI behaviour, documentation, or user training?

### Topic 5.4: Report what changed, not only what shipped

A good wrap-up compares the result with the original target and names the next bottleneck. Example: "We piloted the support assistant with 25 agents. Median resolution time fell from 12 minutes to 7, and escalations stayed within 1 percentage point of normal. The biggest failure type was outdated policy lookups, so the next step is automatic knowledge refresh and tests for lookup quality."

**What to remember:** Delivery is done when you have evidence the original problem improved and the team can operate the system.
