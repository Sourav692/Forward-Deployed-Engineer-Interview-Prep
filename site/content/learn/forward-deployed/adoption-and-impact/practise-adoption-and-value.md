# Practise Adoption and Value


## How to use this practice

These questions start after launch: people are not using the tool, or the value is in dispute. Some ask you to produce a plan, a scorecard or a calculation. Answer aloud first, writing the key items down, then compare with the outline. Check every calculation yourself.

Every customer and figure below is fictional.

> **What you'll practise**
>
> - Writing an adoption plan and a scorecard.
> - Investigating low usage and work done outside the tool.
> - Separating a change's effect from everything else.
> - Calculating realised savings and making a recommendation.

## Adoption plans and scorecards

Taught in [training and champions](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/training-and-champions) and [measuring trust and overrides](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/measuring-trust-and-overrides).

<details>
<summary>Practice question 1. Dunmore Pharmacy launches a prescription assistant in 40 pharmacies next month. Write its first 30-day adoption plan.</summary>

- **Answer:** Before launch: train area managers first, then pharmacists and dispensers inside their own shifts, and name two champions per area. Days 1 to 3: a floor walker in the ten busiest pharmacies, a daily call to collect problems. Week 1: compare use by pharmacy and role, and visit the lowest five. Weeks 2 to 3: area managers review use and spot-check results weekly, small fixes released. Day 30: review against targets set before launch, such as 80% of requests handled in the tool in every area, then decide to hand over, extend support or redesign.
- **Trade-off:** Floor walkers in ten pharmacies cost staff time. Without them, first-week problems go unreported.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "Which pharmacies get walkers?" The busiest, and any with weak connectivity or many locums.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 2. Build an adoption scorecard for Caverly Logistics' exception assistant.</summary>

- **Answer:** Reach: eligible exceptions opened in the tool, target 95%, from logs against the dispatch system. Completion: exceptions closed in the tool, target 90%, against the dispatch system. Overrides: changed drafts per decision, watched by type, no target. Spot-check error: a weekly sample of 100 accepted drafts checked by a senior dispatcher, target under 3%. Decision time by exception type. Outcome: handling time and reopened exceptions against baseline. All split by depot and shift.
- **Trade-off:** Splitting by shift multiplies the rows. Without it, a failing night shift hides in the average.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "Why no override target?" It would reward agreeing or disagreeing for its own sake.

</details>

## Low adoption

Taught in [diagnosing low adoption](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/diagnosing-low-adoption) and [measuring trust and overrides](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/measuring-trust-and-overrides).

<details>
<summary>Practice question 3. What are the common reasons people do not use a new tool that works?</summary>

- **Answer:** Group them. Worth: the problem does not matter enough to them, or the tool does not solve their real pain. Fit: it sits outside their workflow, adds steps or duplicate entry, was never shown to them properly, or nothing rewards its use. Trust: answers are wrong often enough, or slow enough, that the old way feels safer. Investigate in that order.
- **Trade-off:** Checking fit before quality delays model work. Model work first often fixes the wrong thing.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "How do you find out which?" Watch people work, then check the logs by group.
- **Go deeper:** [Diagnosing low adoption](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/diagnosing-low-adoption).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 4. Fallow Foods' system is stable and accurate, but the credit team still keeps its old spreadsheet and does much of the work there. What do you do?</summary>

- **Answer:** Count it first: compare credit notes in the ledger with decisions in the tool, and look for exports. Then sit with the team and ask what the spreadsheet does that the tool does not. Common answers are a case the tool cannot handle, a report someone upstream still wants, or a habit nobody asked them to drop. Fix the gap, move the report into the tool, and agree a date with the process owner to retire the spreadsheet.
- **Trade-off:** Retiring a spreadsheet by order, before the gap is fixed, pushes work into shadow copies.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "The manager wants the spreadsheet kept." Find which output they need and provide it from the tool.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 5. Elstree Council's advisers must type each repair into the assistant and again into the old job system. Use is falling. What do you do?</summary>

- **Answer:** Duplicate entry is a cost users pay on every call. Avoiding the tool is rational. Short term, remove the second entry: have the assistant write the job to the old system through an integration, or pre-fill the old screen. If that takes weeks, agree with the process owner which system is the record and drop entry in the other. Then remeasure use by team.
- **Trade-off:** An integration costs build time. Leaving duplicate entry makes every other adoption effort wasted.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "IT says the integration takes three months." Ask for a pre-fill or a file export first.
- **Go deeper:** [Diagnosing low adoption](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/diagnosing-low-adoption).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 6. Usage is at 25%, three months after launch. Lay out your investigation.</summary>

- **Answer:** Day 1: split use by team, role, shift and case type from the logs, and compare tool counts with the system of record. Days 2 to 3: watch the lowest and highest groups work, and ask what they do instead. Day 4: spot-check accuracy on the cases people skip. Day 5: report the main causes, ranked by how many users each affects, with a fix and owner for each, and a target to remeasure.
- **Trade-off:** A week of investigation delays fixes. Guessing the cause usually means fixing the model when the problem is fit.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "High-use teams share one thing." Find it, such as a champion or a simpler workflow, and copy it.

</details>

## Proving and reporting value

Taught in [realised versus estimated value](/learn/forward-deployed/adoption-and-impact/realised-versus-estimated-value).

<details>
<summary>Practice question 7. Finance asks you to prove the assistant caused the savings. How do you set that up?</summary>

- **Answer:** Before launch: a baseline per unit of work, measured the way you will measure after. Roll out in stages, so teams not yet using the tool form a comparison group. Record every other change in the period. Afterwards, take the difference between the two groups' changes, split by case type, and agree the method and the cost rates with finance before you calculate.
- **Trade-off:** Staged rollout delays some users' benefit. A big-bang launch leaves no comparison.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "We already launched everywhere." Use pre-launch trends, and say the result is weaker evidence.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 8. Fallow Foods' claim handling time fell 40% after launch, but a new photo policy and a seasonal drop in claims happened at the same time. How do you separate the effects?</summary>

- **Answer:** Measure per claim, not team hours. The volume drop then does not count. Split by claim type, since the photo policy mostly changes damaged-goods claims. Compare with the same months last year to judge the seasonal effect on mix. If a team or region lacked the tool, use it as a comparison. Report the tool's share with its uncertainty, and name what you could not control.
- **Trade-off:** A careful split gives a smaller, less exciting number. A 40% claim that falls apart costs more.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "Give me one number." Give the best estimate with a range, and the method beside it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 9. At Elstree Council, the housing team thrives with the assistant while the council tax team struggles. What do you do?</summary>

- **Answer:** Report them separately, never as an average. Compare the two: case types, data quality, workflow, training, champions and how leaders review the work. Watch the struggling team work. If the cause is fixable, such as missing categories or no champion, redesign for them and rerun. If their work does not suit the tool, recommend keeping them on the old process and say so.
- **Trade-off:** A per-team decision is more complex to explain. One average hides both the success and the failure.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "Should housing expand meanwhile?" Yes, if its checklist holds. One team's struggle need not block the other.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 10. Dunmore's assistant saves 1.5 minutes on each of 3,000 requests a day, over 300 days a year, at a loaded cost of £18 an hour. Dunmore cut 6,000 locum hours a year at £22 an hour, and the tool costs £60,000 a year. Work out the three numbers.</summary>

- **Answer:** Estimated: 3,000 × 1.5 = 4,500 minutes, 75 hours a day. Over 300 days, 22,500 hours, or £405,000 at £18. Released: 6,000 hours, 27% of the estimate. Realised: 6,000 × £22 = £132,000 cash, minus £60,000 running cost, so £72,000 net a year.
- **Trade-off:** Reporting £72,000 looks small next to £405,000. It is the figure finance will confirm.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "How do you raise the realised number?" Agree which other spend falls, such as overtime, as volume grows.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Practice question 11. Three months in, Elstree's wrong-trade visits fell from 12% to 9% of 20,000 jobs a year, while a comparison area fell by 0.5 points. Each wrong visit costs £85, and the tool costs £40,000 a year. Use is 80%. What do you recommend?</summary>

- **Answer:** Effect: 3.0 minus 0.5 is 2.5 points, 500 fewer wrong visits a year, worth £42,500. Net of £40,000, about £2,500 a year. The cash case is marginal, though tenants get fewer wasted visits. Recommend continue and redesign: find which repair categories still go wrong and fix them, with a target and a review date. Stop if the next review shows no gain.
- **Trade-off:** Continuing a marginal tool costs effort. Stopping loses an effect that a fix may grow.
- **Follow-up to expect:** "Should it expand?" Not until the net value is clearly positive.

</details>

## Key takeaways

- A 30-day adoption plan has a supported start, gap finding, coaching from data and a review against targets.
- Investigate low use by worth, fit and trust, with logs and observation.
- Duplicate entry and spreadsheets left in place are fit problems, not training problems.
- Isolate the effect with per-unit measures, case-type splits and a comparison group.
- Report estimated, released and realised value, and recommend from evidence.

## Check yourself

<details>
<summary>1. In practice question 10, why is released capacity only 27% of the estimate?</summary>

Dunmore cut 6,000 of the 22,500 hours the tool saved. The rest was spread across shifts and not removed or moved on purpose.

</details>

<details>
<summary>2. In practice question 11, why use 2.5 points and not 3?</summary>

The comparison area fell by 0.5 points without the tool, so only 2.5 points belong to the tool.

</details>
