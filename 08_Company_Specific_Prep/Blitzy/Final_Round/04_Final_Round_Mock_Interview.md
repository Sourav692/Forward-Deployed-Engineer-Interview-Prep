# Final Round Mock Interview and Scorecard

Run this aloud with a colleague or record yourself. The point is not to sound rehearsed. The point is to notice whether your answers are clear, specific, and connected to the customer outcome.

## Before the Mock

Bring your engineering project inventory, three-story capture sheet, and stakeholder evidence sheet from the other final-round guides. Close the source readings during the interview. Have the interviewer choose questions and interrupt with follow-ups.

This is a suggested practice format; Blitzy's email does not specify the final round's duration or exact questions. Alternate between real-experience questions and hypothetical scenarios so you practise both evidence and reasoning.

## Suggested 50-minute format

| Segment | Time | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 3 min | "Tell us about yourself and why this FDE role." |
| Engineering judgement | 12 min | Start with the five-minute real project walkthrough, then choose from questions 1-3 |
| Ownership | 15 min | Use questions 4-6 below |
| Executive communication | 12 min | Use questions 7-8 below |
| Your questions | 5 min | Ask two questions from `03_Questions_To_Ask_Interviewer.md` |
| Reflection | 3 min | Score the answers and identify one to tighten |

## Questions

### Engineering judgement

1. A customer wants to use AI to modernise a critical legacy workflow. How would you choose the first scope?
2. What evidence would you need before trusting an AI-generated change in production?
3. Tell us about a technically difficult problem where you changed course after learning more.

### Ownership

4. Tell us about an ambiguous problem you owned from discovery through delivery.
5. Tell us about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder or senior leader.
6. Tell us about a delivery problem, incident, or mistake. What did you do in the first day, and what changed afterwards?

### Executive communication

7. A CTO wants a quarter-end outcome, but your technical team says the proposed scope is unsafe. How do you handle the conversation?
8. How would you explain the value and limits of Blitzy to a non-technical executive who expects a fully autonomous system?

## Interviewer follow-ups

Ask these after any answer that stays too high level.

- What did you personally decide or do?
- What was the alternative, and why did you reject it?
- How did you know the result was good enough?
- Who disagreed, and how did you bring them with you?
- What would you do differently now?
- What did the customer or executive need from you at that moment?

## Scoring rubric

Score each answer from 1 to 5.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity | Hard to follow | Main point appears | Headline and conclusion are immediate |
| Specificity | Generic principles | Some real detail | Clear stakes, actions, and evidence |
| Technical judgement | Tool list | Mentions a trade-off | Explains evidence, risk, and a safe decision |
| Ownership | Mostly "we" | Your role is present | Your decisions, communication, and accountability are explicit |
| Executive communication | Jargon or status report | Understandable | Outcome, decision, trade-off, and ask are all clear |
| Learning | No reflection | General lesson | Specific change in how you operate now |

An answer below 20 out of 30 needs another pass. Do not add more detail first. Lead with the decision, then cut anything that does not help the interviewer understand your judgement.

## Final self-check

- Did I use real examples, without exposing confidential details?
- Did I name a trade-off rather than presenting every choice as obvious?
- Did I explain how I kept the customer or stakeholder informed?
- Did I distinguish what I did from what the team did?
- Did I make the customer outcome more important than the technical activity?
- Did I leave room for an interviewer to ask a follow-up instead of answering every possible question at once?

## Repair the Weakest Answer With Existing Material

Use this map after scoring. Review one source for five minutes, rewrite the answer's opening and decision, then repeat the answer aloud. Score the repaired answer against the same rubric.

| Weakness | Return to | Repeat this exercise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture answer lists tools without reasoning | Engineering guide's brownfield reading path | Explain one integration choice, its alternative, and the evidence that would change your mind |
| Validation answer stops at "tests pass" | Engineering guide's G13 release-gating case | Name the baseline, risk tier, approval owner, stop condition, and rollback |
| Diagnosis answer guesses the cause | Engineering guide's incident-triage reading path | State the symptom, containment, baseline comparison, and next discriminating test |
| Ownership answer stays at "we" | Ownership guide's ambiguity or delivery-pressure worksheet | Name your responsibility, decision, action, and evidence of the result |
| Pushback answer makes the stakeholder sound unreasonable | Ownership guide's pushback worksheet and executive stakeholder sheet | State their pressure, your options, their response, and the agreement reached |
| Executive answer is a technical status dump | Executive guide's CTO trust reading path | Give the outcome, current fact, recommendation, trade-off, and ask in 45 seconds |

Use the reading paths in the companion guides to open the linked repository sources. Repair one weakness per pass; run a second mock only after the weak answer improves.
